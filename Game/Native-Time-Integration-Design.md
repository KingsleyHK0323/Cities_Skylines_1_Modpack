## Minimal proposed integration contract

These are logical requirements, not existing game APIs or instructions to enable a writer. No new general-purpose framework or diagnostic UI is needed to state them.

1. **Session and authority.** A future host must identify a valid loaded session and an exclusive or conflict-aware commit owner. The current generation, loading flags and cache-local preview owner do not authenticate a city or exclude another assembly. If the actual lifecycle or another writer cannot be handled, remain unavailable. A second controller must not consume the first controller's interval or commit; duplicated assemblies and third-party writers require evidence beyond an in-process lock.
2. **Intent and time.** Accept the selected multiplier and a committed revision at a verified simulation boundary. Sample the monotonic source with its frequency, and establish which entire interval was eligible. A rate, pause, speed or session transition splits or invalidates the relevant interval; it must not be retrospectively assigned the new value.
3. **Native reconciliation.** At a game-verified serialized point, obtain the fields and freshness evidence required by the chosen implementation. Compare the desired progression with the native progression already performed. The existing `Candidate` status and caller-supplied `executionBoundaryVerified`, `noOtherClockWriter` and freshness booleans are dry-run assertions, not such evidence.
4. **Conditional commit.** Only a separately approved implementation may change the specific native state, after its ordering, event behavior, competing writes, interpolation and save interaction are established. Neither a callback name, `QueueSimulationThread`, a public field nor a successful compile establishes a multi-field transaction. The inspected wrapper's `QueueSimulationThread` may invoke inline when already on the simulation thread, otherwise it queues an action; its public-interface availability, ordering, unload behavior and compatibility still need verification.
5. **Acceptance and uncertainty.** Advance the accepted target and the relevant fractional remainders only after a commit is confirmed or a verified no-op is accepted according to a verified contract. If an operation is known not to have committed, reanchor or retry only under the approved policy. If it partly committed, events may have run, or its result is unknown, block further attempts for that session and report uncertainty. Do not automatically retry, restore old values, or claim rollback. Blocking this mod's writes does not itself stop the game's own clock.

An eligible interval can yield less than one whole target tick. A future contract must either accept its fractional remainder as a verified no-op under the same valid session and ownership, or retain an unconsumed confirmed anchor and calculate the cumulative interval later without double counting. It must not repeatedly discard valid fractions. Split and unsplit eligible intervals should yield the same cumulative result under an unchanged rate and compatible frequency; an uncertain or partial commit does not qualify as a confirmed no-op.

Here, no-op means no **module** write; the game may already have advanced natively and that advancement still needs reconciliation. As a mathematical illustration, 60 equally spaced samples per second at the provisional 1x phase target would average about 0.01264 of a phase step per sample, requiring roughly 80 samples to accumulate one step. Repeatedly dropping every fractional sample would prevent progress. This is not an observation of the game's actual cadence.

## Integer remainders and representation

The real-time conversion and the calendar/phase relationship each need explicit remainder accounting; they are not interchangeable. Conceptually, for a counter delta `C`, counter frequency `f` and rational multiplier `n/d`, the target 100-nanosecond ticks are the quotient of `C * n * 10,000,000` plus the prior compatible remainder by `f * d`. An actual implementation must prevent intermediate overflow, respect the selected decimal policy, and define how a rate/frequency transition carries or resets a previously accepted remainder. The formula is a requirement, not executable code or a selected dependency.

Under the **additional unverified assumptions** that `N = 65,536`, one phase step occurs per accepted native frame, and one calendar day equals one phase cycle, `864,000,000,000 / 65,536 = 13,183,593` remainder `49,152`, or `13,183,593.75` ticks per frame. A fixed floor is short by 49,152 ticks (4.9152 ms) per cycle; a fixed ceiling is high by 16,384 ticks (1.6384 ms). As arithmetic only, one frame at 13,183,593 ticks and three at 13,183,594 ticks in each four-frame group sum exactly over 65,536 such frames. This is not a safe-write recipe: varying `P` changes conversions and interpolation, and the game may not execute one eligible frame per desired phase step.

For an unchanged `F`, preserving `F * P + O` while replacing `P` by `P'` would mathematically require `O' = O + F * (P - P')`. That identity neither preserves the independent `R`/`T` display nor proves a valid or atomic native update. `TimeToFrame` also uses the current `P` to convert times. At one phase step per frame, the target implies roughly 0.7585 simulation frames per eligible real second at 1x and 7.5852 at 10x; actual frame cadence is unknown, and slowing the game simulation to those rates is not a selected solution.

### Mechanism and source limits

The inspected reference IL for `SimulationManager.SimulationStep` loads the current metadata date and `m_timePerFrame`, calls `DateTime.op_Addition`, and stores the result before later simulation manager calls. The surrounding fields and call signature, rather than a fixed IL offset, identify a candidate match. See the <!-- [recorded reference IL](../Local_Validation_20261113_CityTime/day-night-il-20261113-01/simulation-manager-il.txt) -->recorded reference IL (local evidence not included in a source-only backup). This is a static observation of that reference, not proof about an executing game.

A Harmony transpiler processes IL when Harmony generates a replacement method. A minimally scoped candidate could replace that addition call with a helper having equivalent stack inputs and return type; a helper needing a manager argument would also require a valid additional stack load. The helper must preserve the original addition in the disabled path. Transpilers are chained and rerun when other transpilers are added, so the input is not necessarily the untouched reference IL. See [Harmony transpilers](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/patching-transpiler.html).

A prefix or postfix surrounds the whole target method and cannot by itself intercept this interior addition at its original position. A prefix can skip the whole original, which also skips its simulation work; a postfix is too late for the manager calls already made. A postfix is not guaranteed after an exception. A finalizer addresses execution-time exceptions but does not undo side effects; installation-time `Cleanup` is a different mechanism and can suppress a patching exception. See [execution flow](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/execution.html) and [auxiliary methods](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/patching-auxiliary.html).

Before any modified instructions are emitted, a candidate must validate the target method and count the complete field-and-call pattern, accepting exactly one match. Zero or multiple matches must prevent activation. Harmony's matcher offers explicit invalid-match checks; it does not establish uniqueness for this application. Instruction labels, exception boundaries and evaluation-stack types must remain valid. Rebuilding a patch chain can change the input again, so a previous successful match is not permanent evidence. See [matching](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/patching-transpiler-matcher.html) and [instructions](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/patching-transpiler-codes.html).

A thrown installation or rebuild error does not establish transactional rollback. Stop activation, inspect the specific patch state and recover only through a verified, owner-scoped procedure; do not blindly retry or remove unrelated patches. Harmony documents priority and before/after constraints for prefix/postfix ordering, while transpilers are chained; load order is not a general serialization guarantee. Patch ownership needs a unique ID. The default `UnpatchAll()` can affect all owners, and even scoped unpatching rebuilds a replacement rather than guaranteeing restoration of prior effects or completion of in-flight calls. See [priorities](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/priorities.html), [unpatching](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/basics.html#unpatching), and the [Harmony API](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/api/HarmonyLib.Harmony.html).

Harmony's documented use requires an available assembly and compatible compile-time/runtime references. The checked [game build](Build-GameAssembly.ps1) and [diagnostic host build](../Diagnostics/ControlledReadOnlyHost/Build-ControlledReadOnlyHost.ps1) list six game references and no Harmony reference. The specifically checked `Resource/Game_Data/0Harmony.dll` and `Resource/Game_Data/Harmony.dll` are absent; this does not establish absence elsewhere. Reflection would still require a usable runtime implementation. The [official requirements](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/basics.html) and [2.4.3 build targets](https://github.com/pardeike/Harmony/blob/v2.4.3/Directory.Build.props) do not prove compatibility with the target Unity/Mono runtime. Inlining, methods without managed IL and other edge cases require version-specific review: [official limitations](https://harmony.pardeike.net/v2/articles/patching-edgecases.html).

### Calendar coherence still to solve

Let `F` be the frame index, `P` the native time per frame, `O` the tick offset, `M` the metadata date and `d` the intended eligible elapsed time for a frame. Assuming the initial relation `M = F * P + O`, replacing only the native `M += P` with `M += d` leaves `FrameToTime(F)` and metadata apart by `P - d` after one increment. For example, the reference initial `P` is 147.65625 seconds; if `d` is 1 second, the mismatch is 146.65625 seconds. This is an arithmetic counterexample, not a measured game frame.

One logical end-state relation would additionally require `O' = O + d - P`. That equation is not a safe implementation: intermediate readers can see partial state, and changing `O` can move the separately interpolated display backward even if the final metadata date is nondecreasing. The earlier display counterexample and inspected save path above remain applicable. A credible implementation needs a proven reader/commit contract covering display interpolation, `FrameToTime`, `TimeToFrame`, the inspected save route, lifecycle interruption and restoration, or a demonstrated equivalent that avoids inconsistent observations. Multiple independently installed patches are not automatically an atomic commit. Event dates may change as a disclosed product effect; preventing a backward current date and partial saves remains necessary.
